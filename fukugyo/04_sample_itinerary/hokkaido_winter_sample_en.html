Why this order works

  • Mountains first, coast later: the highest road of the trip (Mikuni Pass) is crossed on day 2 in full daylight, while you're still fresh for the longest mountain drive.
  • Sleep close enough to the coast: Obihiro is about 1 h 15 min from the beach, so the jewelry ice sunrise (6:28) needs a 4:45 start: the ice glows best in the half hour before sunrise.
  • Made for your phone: every stop has a map link, and the PDF works offline in areas with weak signal.
  • Car where it matters: the coast at dawn is almost impossible by public transport. Each day also shows the train and bus option, so you can decide leg by leg.

* Driving is your decision. Winter tires come standard on Hokkaido rentals, but snowy roads need experience, and you need an International Driving Permit valid in Japan (or an official Japanese translation for some countries). If you have never driven on snow and ice, choose the bus options. Want Biei instead of Sounkyo? Swap days 1–2 for Asahikawa → Biei → Karikachi Pass → Obihiro.

If the weather turns

  • Blizzard warning: don't drive. Swap the day with a rest day in town, or move by train where it runs.
  • No jewelry ice: stay for the sunrise anyway, then spend more time at the Harunire Tree and the Tokachi River mouth.
  • Cranes not at the field: wait for the afternoon feeding, or check other viewing spots in Tsurui.

Hosooka Observation Deck, day 4: the wetland just after sunset.
