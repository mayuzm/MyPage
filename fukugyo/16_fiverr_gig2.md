## FAQ（各300字以内）
**Should I drive or take the train in winter?**
It depends on the route. Trains are reliable on the main lines; a car reaches quiet coasts and wetlands but needs snow-driving experience. I suggest car, train or a mix for each leg and keep drives in daylight where I can. The choice is always yours.

**Can I include ski days?**
Yes, as part of a wider winter trip. I compare resorts (size, beginner terrain, snow, access), plan airport and resort transfers by bus, train or car, mix ski and sightseeing days, and explain lift passes. I don't give slope advice or arrange lessons or guides. A week at one resort may not need me.

**Will I see drift ice, jewelry ice or cranes?**
Nature can't be guaranteed. I plan around the weeks and times of day when they're most likely, and add a backup for each in case they don't show up.

**Do I need an International Driving Permit?**
Japan accepts International Driving Permits issued under the 1949 Geneva Convention (e.g. from the US, UK, Australia or Canada). Licenses from Switzerland, Germany, France, Belgium, Monaco and Taiwan need an official Japanese translation instead. Get it before you leave home.

**Do you book hotels, cars or tours?**
No. I tell you which areas to stay in, what to book and by when. You book everything yourself.

**What about the March timetable change?**
Japan revises many train and bus schedules in mid-March. For trips after that, I plan with the current timetable and mark the times to recheck once the new one is out.

**Can you include Tokyo or other regions?**
This service is for Hokkaido. If your trip also covers other areas, message me first, or use my Japan itinerary review gig (on my profile) for the rest of your plan.

**How do revisions work?**
Basic includes 1 revision, Standard and Premium include 2, within the same trip. New dates or extra days count as a new order.

**When should I order?**
Ideally 4 weeks or more before your trip, since delivery takes 10 to 21 days. If it's sooner, message me first. I reply within 24 hours, usually at lunchtime or in the evening (Japan time).
